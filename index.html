Guides

Straightforward answers to the questions every New Hampshire buyer and seller asks.

Buying a Home in New Hampshire

Start with a pre-approval so you know your budget before you fall for a house — and keep in mind that property taxes vary widely from town to town, so the same price can mean very different monthly payments depending on where you buy.

Many New Hampshire homes have wells and septic systems rather than town water and sewer. Budget for inspections of both, and ask about the age and service history of the systems before you write an offer.

Selling in Goffstown & Manchester

Pricing starts with comparable sales — what similar homes nearby actually sold for, not what they were listed at. A home priced to the comps draws more showings and stronger offers than one priced on hope.

Small, visible fixes usually pay better than big renovations: fresh paint, a clean entry, and a tidy yard do more for first impressions than a new kitchen most buyers would want to choose themselves.

New Hampshire Property Taxes, Plainly

New Hampshire has no income tax and no sales tax. Local services — schools, roads, police and fire — are funded largely through local property taxes, which is why tax bills here look high compared with other states.

Tax rates are set town by town, so two similar homes in neighboring towns can carry very different annual bills. Always check the current tax rate and the assessed value before you buy, and remember that assessments are updated on a regular cycle.
